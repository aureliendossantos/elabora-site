This page says what data Elabora processes, why, on what basis, for how long, and how to exercise your rights. It covers the `elabora.studio` site, author accounts and the organisations' dashboard (`account.elabora.studio`), publishing and review (`api.elabora.studio`, `revue.elabora.studio`), team projects, the online editor (`app.elabora.studio`), the connector for AI (`mcp.elabora.studio`), the courses published on `<handle>.elabora.page` and the desktop app.

**In short:**

- The service's data (accounts, publications, reviews, team projects) is stored in the European Union.
- Elabora runs no advertising and no audience measurement, and keeps no log of visits to courses.
- Only a team's members read its projects. Elabora does not read their content (section 3).
- Published courses set a single technical cookie, which identifies nobody: it is used to clear what a course kept in your browser after a year, or when the address changes hands (section 5).
- In a browser, « Sign out » erases from the device the copy of your projects and your ElevenLabs key (section 5).
- The desktop app and the online editor send no usage statistics. A text of your course goes to an AI or voice service only if you ask for it (section 8).
- An AI such as Claude or ChatGPT reads your team projects only if you allow it, on an Elabora page (section 8).
- The app's chat and its short tasks only use models whose providers do not keep your texts and do not use them to train their models (section 8).

## 3. Publications, reviews and team projects

**Publications.** An author publishes courses on their space, publicly or privately (visible only through a review). We host the course's files and metadata (title, description, languages, version) and measure the space used. Legal basis: the one the author determines for the data they entrust to us, and performance of the contract for running the hosting. How long: until the author takes the publication down or deletes their account; an unfinished upload expires after 24 hours. A public course can be read by anyone who knows its address.

**Reviews.** An author can open a review on a private publication and share its secret link. Anyone with the link can read the course; to comment you must be at least **15**, with no account. The author runs the review and is responsible for it; Elabora processes the data for them.

| Data | Why | How long |
|---|---|---|
| The name or pseudonym given with the first comment, a hash of the reviewer token | Signing your comments, letting you edit or delete your own | Until the review or the author's account is deleted, and at most **12 months** after the review is closed |
| Comments: text, the place in the course, version, dates, state, replies | Showing your remarks to the author and reviewers, in the right place | The same; a deleted comment loses its text at once |
| The review: title, state, versions sent | Running the review | Until it is deleted, or **12 months** after it is closed |

Your comments are seen by the author and by the people who have the link. The author may give them to their AI assistant: they must then tell you so. Do not write sensitive information in a comment. You can edit or delete your own while the review is open; after that, ask the author or us.

**Organisations and teams.** An organisation groups teams, their seats and their media storage. We keep its name, its owner, its administrators, its seats and its limits. For a team, we keep its name, its members (their account, their role, when they joined) and its invitations. An invitation keeps a hash of the invited address, never the address itself, with the role offered, who invited and the dates. It expires after **14 days**. Elabora sends no e-mail for an invitation: you tell the person yourself.

**Team projects.** A project shared with a team is stored on our servers, in the European Union: its files, its media, its cover picture, its title, its size and the log of its changes. The log says who made each change and when. For a change made by an AI, it says the AI's name and the person it works for. While you work in a project, the other members who are online see that you are there, on which screen or view, and what you selected. The server keeps only the list of who is online, replaced each time someone arrives or leaves.

**Who sees what.**

- A team's members see its projects and the other members' display names (or handles), never their e-mail address.
- A team's owner sees pending invitations, without the address. Nobody learns whether an address has an account.
- An organisation's owner and administrators manage its teams and their members. They do not read the projects of a team they are not a member of.
- Elabora does not read the projects' content: not the files, not the media, not what the changes contain. To run the service, we see a project's title and size, the dates and authors of its changes, and who is connected. To help you in a project, we ask to be invited into the team, with an account the team sees among its members.

**How long.** A project stays until its team deletes it, a team as long as it has a member, an organisation until its owner deletes it. When you leave a team, your changes stay under your name in its projects until your account is deleted. Deleting a project erases its files, its log, its cover and the media nothing else uses. Deleting a team erases all its projects. The backup copies keep the files and log of an erased project for **30 days** (section 7). **Legal basis:** the one the organisation determines for the projects' content; performance of the contract for organisations, teams, their members and the log.

## 4. A space's address

A space's address, `<handle>.elabora.page`, can change **once every 30 days**, with no redirect: the old address stops serving the courses at once.

A handle that is left, by a change or by deleting the account, is **reserved for a year**, then someone else may take it. The names of the publications shown under a handle can never be used there again by anyone else: an old link (in an LMS, an e-mail, a document) then leads to a page not found, not to someone else's course.

For this we keep a hash of the handle during its year of reservation, and a hash of each pair of handle and publication name, with no time limit. These hashes are not linked to the account, but they can be matched with a known handle: they are therefore personal data. **Legal basis:** our legitimate interest in protecting learners and authors' links.

## 5. What is stored in your browser

**On published courses (`<handle>.elabora.page`).**

- **If the author turned on resuming**, the course keeps in your browser (`localStorage`) the screen reached, the status, the score and the state of activities, including what you write in them. Nothing is sent to Elabora or tied to an account. The course tells you so and lets you « Clear my progress ». This progress is **erased a year after your last visit**.
- **Elabora sets one technical cookie** when a page opens. It holds a random number, the same for every visitor of that address: it does not identify you and cannot track you. Courses' scripts cannot read it. It lasts **a year**, renewed at each visit. When your browser comes without it, or with the one of a former owner of the address, Elabora asks it to erase everything that address kept there, before the page shows. This is what guarantees the year of retention, and that a new owner of the address cannot read anything their former learners left. An address with no space (reserved or free) erases in the same way.
- **A course may contain its author's scripts.** What they store is the author's responsibility (section 1). That data is erased in the same cases.

**On the review page (`revue.elabora.studio`)**, the browser keeps the review link's token, your reviewer token and two display preferences. They stay until you erase them (« Forget this review on this device », or the browser's settings); the token of a link that is no longer valid is forgotten.

**In the online editor (`app.elabora.studio`).**

- **Technical cookies** keep you signed in. The pages' scripts cannot read them.
- **To work offline**, the browser keeps a copy of the projects you open, with their media and the changes not sent yet, and your preferences. A copy opens only for the account that made it.
- **Your ElevenLabs key**, if you give one, is kept by a page of its own, `keys.elabora.studio`, which the editor cannot read. It stays in memory for the visit, or in the browser if you tick « Keep the key in this browser ». It is forgotten when you sign out, when your session ends, or when another account signs in in this browser.
- **The player** keeps your display settings and a preview's progress, on the addresses that show your courses (`canvas.elabora.page` and `frames.elabora.page`).
- **The editor's files and the models** for voice and alignment it downloads also stay in the browser, so they need not be downloaded again. They hold nothing of yours.

**« Sign out » erases all of this**, except the editor's files and the models: the copies of the projects, your preferences, your ElevenLabs key and what the player keeps. If the browser did not erase everything, the editor finishes erasing at the next start, before opening a course. If your session ends another way (it expires, or you remove it from another device), the copies of the projects stay in the browser; they do not open for another account. On a shared computer, remember to sign out.

**On the organisations' dashboard (`account.elabora.studio`) and on the connector's consent page (`mcp.elabora.studio`)**, technical cookies keep you signed in, or tie the consent form to your browser while you fill it in.

**No consent banner.** The law (article 82 of the French Data Protection Act, implementing article 5(3) of the ePrivacy directive) exempts from consent what is strictly necessary for the service you ask for. That is the case here: resuming a course where you left it, reaching a review by its link, signing in to the online editor, the dashboard and the connector, the copy of your projects for working offline, and Elabora's cookie on courses, which protects what the course keeps on your device and limits how long. None of them is used for advertising or audience measurement. You can erase all of it in your browser's settings.

## 6. Legal obligations and preventing abuse

**Identifying the authors of content.** French law (the LCEN and [decree no. 2021-1362](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000044228912)) requires keeping certain data that identifies who created content, to the extent the service collects it. Legal basis: legal obligation.

| Category | At Elabora | Legal period |
|---|---|---|
| Identity provided | E-mail address | 5 years after the contract ends |
| Other account information | Handle, display name | 1 year after the account is closed |
| Technical data of a contribution | Whatever the service collects | 1 year after the operation |

This data serves that purpose only and is disclosed only to authorised authorities. Deleting an account does not erase it before its due date.

**Limits against abuse.** To limit code guessing and mass comments, the service counts requests per IP address, turned into a hash. The counter stays about a minute in memory at our host (section 7), then disappears. Code requests are also counted per e-mail address, over 24 hours. Legal basis: legitimate interest (security).

## 7. E-mails, providers and transfers

**The e-mails we send** (sign-in and account deletion codes, the warning before an inactive account is deleted) go through an e-mail sending service, from the European Union. It keeps each message (address, subject, content with the sign-in code, delivery state) for **30 days**, then erases it. It may keep an address that rejects e-mails in a suppression list. Open and click tracking is off.

**The e-mails you send us** (contact@, abuse@, security@, postmaster@) go through Cloudflare to the publisher's mailbox, with a European mail provider. We keep them **3 years** after the last exchange, **5 years** for a report and the decision taken. Legal basis: legitimate interest in answering you; legal obligation for reports and requests from authorities.

| Provider | Role | Where | Safeguards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cloudflare (a US company) | Hosting the site, the API, the online editor, the connector, the review page and the courses; storage, team projects included; receiving e-mail | Storage in the EU; requests go through the data centre nearest the visitor, worldwide | Data processing agreement; Data Privacy Framework; standard contractual clauses |
| E-mail sending service (a US company) | Sending sign-in codes and warnings | Sent from the EU; company and tools in the United States | Data processing agreement; Data Privacy Framework; standard contractual clauses |
| The publisher's e-mail provider | Mailbox | Europe | GDPR-compliant contract |

We only entrust data to providers bound to us by a data processing agreement and, outside the European Union, by one of these safeguards. Cloudflare also processes some data on its own account, to protect its network. The backup copies of the database and of each team project keep erased data for **30 days**. The Data Privacy Framework is the European Commission's adequacy decision of 10 July 2023 for certified US companies.

## 8. The desktop app, the online editor and the connector

Your courses stay on your computer, except the projects you share with a team (section 3). The app sends no usage statistics and no crash reports.

**Our guarantees.**

- A text of your course goes to an AI or voice service only when you ask for it: by choosing that AI and writing to it, or by a gesture of yours. The first gesture that sends a text to an AI you have not chosen asks for your consent first.
- When the chat's agent wants to spend your voice or sound quota, read a file outside the course or read a page of a website, it asks you first. For websites, you can allow that site only, or every site, once and for all; Settings › Tools for AI removes these permissions.
- The chat and its short tasks (the ✨ button beside an empty field, which describes an image or translates a text) only use models whose providers do not keep your texts and do not use them to train their models. Elabora checks this before adding a model, and removes a model whose terms change.
- Elabora receives neither your requests to the chat or its tasks nor the answers: they go from your computer to the AI service.

**What goes over the network.**

| What | To | When |
|---|---|---|
| ElevenLabs voices and sounds: your key, the texts to say, the settings | ElevenLabs, under your account and its terms | Only on a gesture (yours, or your AI assistant's if you allow it) |
| The request given to your outside AI assistant (Claude, Codex) | Its app, then its provider, under your subscription | When you hand it a task |
| The chat: your request, the course's texts and files the agent reads, the results of Elabora's tools and its instructions to the agent. A short task: the text to translate, or the image to describe and the words of its screen | A free AI service, with no account or key | At each request, while the agent works on it; at each task you start |
| Web pages the agent reads for your request | Each page's site, from your computer | When the agent needs them and you have allowed that site. The chat does no web search |
| The programs and models the app downloads (the chat's agent, voice, alignment, transcription) | Public software and model repositories | When you download them, write to the chat or start a short task for the first time; to update them, if automatic updates are on |
| Publishing, review and team projects (sections 2 to 4) | `api.elabora.studio` | When you sign in, publish, manage a review or work in a team project |
| Checking for and downloading updates | The public repository of Elabora's versions | At launch, then every four hours; the download, if automatic updates are on |
| The list of models the chat offers | `elabora.studio` | When a conversation or a task starts, at most once every ten minutes |

These services may be outside the European Union. The AI service of the chat and its tasks processes your requests, with your IP address, on its own account and under its own terms. File requests carry only the IP address and the software, and Elabora's version for downloads; those who host them process them on their own account. The models list is asked for with no parameter and no cookie, and Elabora keeps no log of it. **Legal basis for version checks:** our legitimate interest in telling you about new versions and in the app's security. Settings › Updates stops automatic downloads, not the checks.

On your computer stay your projects (and the copy of the team projects you open there), their history and backups, your ElevenLabs key and your Elabora session (in a file readable by you only, or in the system keychain), generated voices, downloaded models, the chat's conversations (in the course's folder, `.elabora/chats/`), the chat agent's data and an app log. The log may contain file paths and course titles; it is never sent automatically.

**The online editor.** In a browser, the editor talks to Elabora only: to `api.elabora.studio` for your account and your projects, and to `models.elabora.studio` for the voice and alignment models. Elabora serves these models itself and keeps no log of them. The online editor sends no usage statistics and no crash reports. It has no chat: in a browser, your AI goes through the connector. ElevenLabs voices go through the key's page, `keys.elabora.studio`, the only one that talks to ElevenLabs: it sends it your key, the texts to say and the settings, and only when you use its voices or sounds.

**The connector for Claude and ChatGPT.** The connector (`mcp.elabora.studio`) lets an AI on the web, such as Claude or ChatGPT, read and change your teams' projects.

- **Nothing happens without your consent.** The first time, the AI sends you to an Elabora page. You sign in there with a code, then you see the AI's name, your teams and what it will be able to do. It can always read. It can change things only if you leave that box ticked, and only where you are an editor or an owner.
- **It can neither publish, share, invite nor delete a project.** Each change carries its name and yours, and can be undone.
- **What it reads goes to the provider of that AI**, under your account and its terms: the projects' texts, plan and images, your teams' names, and, in the history, the display name of whoever made each change. You choose that service. Elabora receives only what the AI asks of its tools, never your conversations with it.
- **When a member connects their AI**, it reads their teams' projects, including what the other members wrote there.
- **To revoke its access**, remove its device from your account.

**What the connector keeps.** Your consent (your account, the AI's name, the rights given, the tokens as hashes), for up to **30 days** without use, or until the device is removed. For **7 days**: the state of each project at the AI's last read (a version number and identifiers, not the text), so that it does not overwrite what changed since, and its refused writes (the account, the AI, the project, the tool, the error code, never the text). For each call, a log line (the tool, the project, the outcome, the time taken), which our host keeps for **7 days**. Your e-mail address is not there. **Legal basis:** performance of the contract; our legitimate interest for the log and the refused writes, which serve to fix the connector.

## 9. Your rights

You can ask for access to your data, its rectification, its erasure, the restriction of a processing, its portability, and object to processing based on our legitimate interest (sections 2, 4, 6, 7 and 8). You can also give instructions about your data after your death.

Write to **contact@elabora.studio**, from your account's address if you have one; a reviewer gives the review and the name used. We answer within a month, which may be extended by two months if the request warrants it. For data in a review or a course, we pass the request on to the author; for data in a team project, to its organisation.

**Deleting your account**: Settings › Account › Delete my account…, with a code sent to your address; or write to us. Your space, its publications, your reviews and their comments, your sessions and your invitation are deleted, and so are the teams and the organisation you are the only member of. If you are the only owner of a team where other people work, or the owner of an organisation where other people work, the deletion waits: first hand the team or the organisation over to someone else, or delete it. In the projects of the teams you worked in, your name leaves the log; your changes stay there, under an identifier that no longer carries your name. What remains, for the periods given above: identification data the law requires, the backup copies (30 days), the e-mails kept by the sending service (30 days), your handle's reservation for a year and the hashes of your publications' names. The courses on your computer stay there.

**Complaints**: you can contact the CNIL (https://www.cnil.fr/fr/plaintes; 3 place de Fontenoy, TSA 80715, 75334 Paris Cedex 07, France) or your own data protection authority.

## 10. Minors

An account and review comments are for people aged **15 or over**. A person with parental authority can write to us about their child's data; we check their identity and capacity proportionately.

**If you are 15 to 17:** a public course can be seen by everyone, so do not put your full name, your address or photos in it; in a review, a first name or a nickname is enough; if something bothers you, talk to an adult or write to **abuse@elabora.studio**.

## 11. Security and changes

Exchanges are encrypted (HTTPS), secrets are kept only as hashes, and the courses' domain holds no secret. To report a vulnerability: **security@elabora.studio**.

Each version of this page is dated. If an important change affects account holders, we tell them by e-mail before it applies.
