## The difference in one minute

Rise is a web app. You stack ready-made blocks, text, images, accordions, quizzes, and the course adapts to every screen. It is quick to learn, and it lives in your Articulate 360 account.

Elabora is a desktop app for macOS and Windows. You describe the course, the AI proposes a plan, you approve it, then it designs the course to measure and builds the screens, as a scrolling page or as slides, checking each one as it goes. Each course is a folder of files on your computer.

## Rise’s strengths

- **Working as a team.** With Articulate 360 Teams, authors edit different lessons of the same course at the same time, share folders and reuse block templates.
- **Templates and media.** More than 1,000 course templates and the Content Library.
- **Accessibility you can cite.** Articulate states that published Rise courses support WCAG 2.1 AA and lists the screen readers it supports.

## Where Elabora differs

- **Designed to measure, in less of your time.** Your AI designs each course for its audience, its look, illustrations and layouts included, then builds the screens from a plan you approve. Your time goes to the content and the choices. The Claude app, the ChatGPT app on your computer or Elabora’s free chat does the work; each change becomes a card you review and can undo.
- **Your files, for good.** A Rise course lives on Articulate’s servers and stops being editable when the subscription ends. An Elabora course is a folder in an open format, and Elabora is free.
- **More than blocks.** 3D scenes you can operate, dialogues where learners answer characters who speak, narration timed to the screen, with ElevenLabs or free voices made on your computer.
- **Your own AI subscription.** Elabora works with the Claude or ChatGPT plan you already have, so you use AI as much as that plan allows, well beyond the AI credits included in Articulate 360. It does ask you to have that subscription; Elabora’s free chat covers smaller edits.
- **Interactions of any kind.** When a screen needs more than the built-in blocks, your AI writes a free object in JavaScript: a 3D simulation, a mini-game or a custom interaction, sandboxed, tested, and settable by you without code.
- **Peace of mind.** After each change, your AI reads Elabora’s checks and fixes what they find on its own, often before you notice: accessibility (contrast, text alternatives, keyboard use, captions), learning design, and the rules of common LMSs, which each package is tested against in an LMS emulator.
- **Learning design, checked as it builds.** Elabora’s checks apply research on how people learn. Richard Mayer’s principles of multimedia learning keep cognitive load in check: short segments, bounded text, narration and on-screen text that complement each other. Learners get something to do every few screens, feedback that explains each answer, and objectives they can show. An employee who runs training without being an instructional designer is guided as well as a designer.
- **Reviews in place.** Reviews are free: your reviewers comment from a link, without an account. Their comments come back into Elabora, placed on the screen, the block or the word of narration; you reply and resolve them there, as with Review 360 in Rise, or hand them to your AI.
- **Quick to show on a slow connection.** Before its first picture, a Rise course loads 10 font files and 1.5 to 1.7 MB of JavaScript. On slow 4G, the Rise courses we measured showed their content after 14.5 to 23.6 s, Elabora’s examples after 2.8 to 3.8 s ([the study](/en/compare/rise-storyline-slow-to-load/)).

## Coming from Rise

1. Export a course from Rise as a web or SCORM package.
2. In Elabora, create a course and attach the ZIP: the import is in beta, and its report tells you what came over.
3. Keep Rise for the courses your team edits together.
