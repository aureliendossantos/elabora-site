## The difference in one minute

Storyline is a slide editor. You place every object, then add layers, states and triggers to make it react. It gives you control over each pixel and each click, and it takes the time that this control asks for.

Elabora starts from what your learners need to be able to do. You describe your audience and attach your documents; the AI proposes a plan of objectives, parts and screens; you approve it, then it designs the course to measure and builds the screens: their layout, their quizzes, their 3D scenes and dialogues. As it goes, it reads Elabora’s checks and fixes what they find. You refine them by asking the agent, or by editing each block yourself. The course ends as a package in the format your LMS expects, or as a web link.

## Storyline’s strengths

- **Software simulations.** Screen recording that you can edit afterwards, in demo, trial and assessment modes.
- **Slides shared in a team.** With Articulate 360 Teams, members share slides with each other (Team Slides).
- **A large ecosystem.** Content Library 360 with millions of photos, illustrations, icons and videos, years of tutorials and a large community.

## Where Elabora differs

- **Designed to measure, in less of your time.** Your AI designs each course for its audience, its look, illustrations and layouts included, then builds the screens from a plan you approve. Your time goes to the content and the choices. The Claude app, the ChatGPT app on your computer or Elabora’s free chat does the work, and each change shows up as a card you review, undone in one click.
- **Behaviours your AI writes.** Storyline gives you layers, states, triggers and variables to set slide by slide, and its AI Chat can now create triggers and variables. In Elabora, your AI writes each screen’s events, layers and variables, which read as sentences you can retouch.
- **Interactions of any kind.** When a screen needs more than the built-in blocks, your AI writes a free object: its own JavaScript, such as a 3D simulation, a mini-game or a custom interaction, sandboxed, tested, and settable by you without code. It plays the part of Storyline’s web objects, with the whole web platform to work with, and your AI writes it.
- **Your own AI subscription.** Elabora works with the Claude or ChatGPT plan you already have, so you use AI as much as that plan allows, well beyond the AI credits included in Articulate 360. It does ask you to have that subscription; Elabora’s free chat covers smaller edits.
- **On Mac and Windows.** Elabora is a desktop app on both. On a Mac you need no virtual machine, no Parallels subscription and no Windows licence.
- **The format your LMS expects.** Elabora picks SCORM 1.2 or SCORM 2004 from what your client’s LMS documents, and you can choose cmi5 or xAPI in the course’s settings.
- **Your files stay readable.** A course is a folder on your computer, in an open format with public schemas. Elabora is free, so your courses stay editable for good.
- **Peace of mind.** After each change, your AI reads Elabora’s checks and fixes what they find on its own, often before you notice: accessibility (contrast, text alternatives, keyboard use, captions), learning design, and the rules of LMSs such as Moodle, Cornerstone and SuccessFactors, which each package is tested against in an LMS emulator.
- **Learning design, checked as it builds.** Elabora’s checks apply research on how people learn. Richard Mayer’s principles of multimedia learning keep cognitive load in check: short segments, bounded text, narration and on-screen text that complement each other. Learners get something to do every few screens, feedback that explains each answer, and objectives they can show. An employee who runs training without being an instructional designer is guided as well as a designer.
- **Reviews down to the word, inside Elabora.** Reviews are free: your reviewers comment from a link, without an account. Their comments come back into Elabora, placed on the screen, the block or the word of narration; you reply and resolve them there, as with Review 360 in Storyline, or hand them to your AI.
- **Quick to show on a slow connection.** On slow 4G, the Storyline courses we measured showed their content after 14.6 to 24.3 s, with 0.8 to 1.2 MB of JavaScript; Elabora’s examples after 2.8 to 3.6 s, with 0.14 to 0.38 MB ([the study](/en/compare/rise-storyline-slow-to-load/)).

## Coming from Storyline

1. Keep Storyline for the courses your team maintains in Storyline, or that rely on software simulations.
2. Try one course in Elabora: create a new one from your brief, or import a .story project (beta) and read its import report.
3. Ask your AI to test the package for your client’s LMS, then upload it there.
