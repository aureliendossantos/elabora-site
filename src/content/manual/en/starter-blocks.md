Elabora has few blocks, and each one comes in several forms. A block stands for one learner action and one shape of data: opening panels, following steps, exploring a picture, answering a question. Presentations only change the look: going from **Tabs** to **Cards** does not touch a single word.

This page follows the block list, family by family, with the entries as you see them: **Text**, **Media**, **Layout**, **Interaction** and **Questions**. It also says what ties them together: **Transform into…**, **After exploring** and the **Opening** layout. To add a block, see [screens and blocks](/en/docs/screens-and-blocks/#adding-a-block). The reasons behind these choices are in [the learning science behind Elabora](/en/docs/learning-science/#revealing-is-not-practice).

## How they work

- **Each entry is one thing you have in mind.** You are looking for flip cards, a timeline, a matching exercise: each has its own entry in the list, with no indentation and no submenu. Entries marked with an ellipsis (**Image…**, **Gallery…**, **Scrolling…**) ask for your images first.
- **The variants beside the preview are styles of that thing.** **Flip cards** come as a **Grid** or **One at a time**; a **Callout** as **Information**, **Tip**, **Warning** or **Important**. Click the one you want and the block arrives that way.
- **The inspector offers every presentation.** Once the block is inserted, it keeps the block’s name (**Panels**, **Sort**, **Steps**…) and **Presentation** is the first setting in **Properties**, a choice in buttons. You go from **Cards** to **Tabs**, or from **Pairs** to **Categories**, whenever you like.
- **Only the settings that apply show.** A setting used by one presentation only (the title of an image with **Text beside**, the date of a **Timeline**) is shown for that presentation alone.
- **Nothing moves.** A block that shows one thing at a time (tabs, cards, steps) reserves the room of its tallest item: the page does not jump when the learner changes item.
- **Calm by construction.** No marker pulses, every movement has a quieter form when the learner asks for less motion, and tabs become panels to unfold in a narrow window.
- **Searching by another name.** The block list’s search field understands Rise’s names, and each one leads to its entry: “flashcard” and “flip” find **Flip cards**, “labeled graphic” the **Interactive image**, “process” **Steps**, “timeline” the **Timeline**, “matching” **Match**, “sorting” **Categorize**, “banner” the **Image** as a band.

## Text

The family holds **Text**, **Title**, **Lesson introduction**, **Callout**, **Statement**, **Divider**, **Equation**, **Quote** and **Persona**. **Quote** and **Persona** close the family, under a quotation-mark icon.

**Text** has one more setting, **Margin note**: a remark that goes in the margin in a wide window and after the text in a narrow one. **Callout** comes as **Information**, **Tip**, **Warning** or **Important**.

**Statement** sets the one idea of the screen to remember in large type, as **Large**, **Between rules** or **Over an image**. It is Rise’s *statement*.

- **Alignment**: **Centred** or **At the start**.
- **Over an image** puts the text on the theme’s scrim, and the text’s contrast is measured against the picture behind it.
- The checks flag a statement that repeats a text of the same screen.

**Quote** (*quote* in Rise) gives the floor to someone: **Large**, **Between rules**, **With portrait** or **Over an image**. It starts with an **Author** and a **Role** to replace; **With portrait** and **Over an image** ask for the image first. In the inspector, a quote is a **Statement** that has an author: **Author**, **Role** and **Portrait** make a statement a quote, in all its presentations. **Quotation mark**, one more presentation, is only chosen there.

**Divider** marks a pause between two parts of a long page: **Line**, **Ornament** (the theme’s drawing), **Number** or **Space**. A **Number** counts the screen’s dividers; **Count from 01 again** restarts the count. **Space** is **Small**, **Medium** or **Large**. Screen readers skip dividers.

**Lesson introduction**, right after **Title**, is only offered on a screen without a layout: it gives it the **Opening** layout (see below). **Persona** is a picture, who they are and what they say: it asks for the image first.

## Media

The family holds **Image…**, **Gallery…**, **Compare two images…**, **Video…** and **Audio…**. Each asks first for a file from your computer.

**Image…** is placed as **Centred**, **Wide**, **Band** (edge to edge), **Text beside** or **Text over it** (*banner*, *image & text*, *text on image* in Rise).

- **Focal point**: click the picture where its subject is. Crops (the band, a phone) keep it in view. **Centre** puts it back in the middle.
- **Opens full size**: a click enlarges the image in the player, with its caption.
- **Text beside**: **Image side** (**Left** or **Right**) and **Image width** (**Half**, **A third**, **Two thirds**), with a title and a text. **Text over it** and **Band** have a **Height** (**Short**, **Medium**, **Tall**). Keep the text over an image to a sentence or two.
- **Band** is an **Edge to edge** block: a block’s **Width** has four choices, **Full**, **Wide**, **Half** and **Edge to edge**. On a slide, the edge is the slide’s.

**Gallery…** (*gallery* in Rise) asks for your images, then lays them out as a **Grid**, a **Mosaic** or **One at a time**. Each image has its caption; **Columns** and **Shape** set the grid; **Opens full size** opens images large, with previous and next. **One at a time** never turns by itself.

**Compare two images…** is a slider that wipes from one image to the other, before and after. In the inspector it is the **Gallery**, and its **Presentation** offers all four: **Grid**, **Mosaic**, **One at a time** and **Before and after**.

**Video…** and **Audio…** have not changed: see [images, video and audio](/en/docs/screens-and-blocks/#images-video-and-audio).

## Layout

The family holds **Stack**, **Columns** and **Do / Don’t**.

**Stack** shows contents that follow each other in the same place.

**Columns** (*columns* in Rise) puts blocks side by side: **2 columns** or **3 columns**. Each column says how many blocks it holds; **Add a paragraph** puts one more in. **Widths**: **Equal**, **First wider** or **Last wider**; **Alignment**: **Top** or **Centred**. On a phone the columns stack. With two columns, the third one’s content is kept but not shown.

**Do / Don’t**, right after **Columns**, is two columns already filled in: what to do in a **Tip** callout, what to avoid in a **Warning** callout.

## Interaction

The family holds **Accordion**, **Tabs**, **Flip cards**, **Myth or fact?**, **Steps**, **Timeline**, **Scrolling…**, **Interactive image…**, **Guided tour…**, **Character**, **Dialogue**, **Decision** and **Button**. It is read by what the learner does.

### Panels

**Accordion**, **Tabs** and **Flip cards** are three entries of one block, **Panels** in the inspector (Rise’s accordion, tabs and flashcards: *accordion*, *tabs*, *flashcards*). It groups titled items that learners open in whatever order they like.

- **Presentation**, in the inspector: **Accordion** (the default), **Tabs**, **Cards** or **One card at a time**. **Flip cards** are placed as a **Grid** or **One at a time**.
- Each item has a title, a text and an image. On a card, the front is the title and the image, the back the text.
- **Think first**: a prompt shown before revealing (“Before you turn each card over, say what you think is on the back”). It turns a click into recall: why, see [think first](/en/docs/learning-science/#think-first) and [flip cards as retrieval](/en/docs/learning-science/#flip-cards-as-retrieval).
- **Say “Turn the card over”** sets the cards’ hint.
- Tabs turn into panels to unfold in a narrow window. The checks flag more items than the presentation shows well (over 7 tabs, over 12 cards) and a tab label that is too long (over 24 characters).
- **Myth or fact?**, right after **Flip cards**, is a deck already filled in: the front asks, the back answers, with a prompt that asks learners to decide before turning. Searching “key words”, “vocabulary” or “glossary” finds **Flip cards** too.

### Steps

**Steps**, **Timeline** and **Scrolling…** are three entries of one block, **Steps** in the inspector (*process* and *timeline* in Rise). It reads a procedure or a series of events step by step. Do not confuse it with the **Process** chart, which is read at a glance, with short labels.

- **Steps**: “Step 2 of 5”, previous and next, the keyboard’s arrows and a swipe; a step that has been shown counts as seen. **Introduction** and **Summary** open and close the series. It never moves on by itself. In the inspector, this is **One at a time**.
- **Timeline**: a vertical timeline, with a **Date** per event. Each event comes in as it reaches the screen.
- **Scrolling…** asks for your images first and makes one step per image. A figure stays in place while the texts scroll past; scrolling remains the learner’s own, never taken over. **Figure**: **Each its picture**, **Layers** (the images pile up) or **Outlined zones** (the view moves over a detail of **Figure’s picture**; each step has its zone, which you draw on the picture and move with the mouse or the arrows, Shift and the arrows resize it). Without scrolling support, or with reduced motion, each step’s image shows with its step. In slides, a check warns you that scrolling steps are out of place.
- **Number the steps** puts a number on each one. See [steps, or scrolling](/en/docs/learning-science/#steps-or-scrolling).

### Interactive image

**Interactive image…** and **Guided tour…** are two entries of one block, **Interactive image** in the inspector (*labeled graphic* in Rise). Each asks for the picture first, then puts markers on it that open what they explain.

- **Interactive image…**: **By the marker**, **At the side** or **Under the picture**.
- **Guided tour…**: the markers one after the other, in order. In the inspector it is a fourth presentation, **Guided tour**.
- Click the picture to add a marker; drag it to move it. Each marker has a title, a text and may have an image.
- **Markers show**: **Numbers** or **Icon** (**Plus**, **Information**, **Question**, **Eye**). **Instruction**: the sentence that says what to do (“Select each marker to learn more”).
- Markers are at least 44 px and never pulse; Escape closes a panel. In a narrow window they become a numbered list under the picture. A check flags markers that overlap on a phone, and another more than 8 markers.
- Why the words stay next to what they describe: [words next to their picture](/en/docs/learning-science/#words-next-to-their-picture).

### Character, Dialogue, Decision, Button

**Character** shows a character of the course who says or thinks a sentence or two: **Speech** or **Thought**. Choose one among the course’s characters, or let Elabora create one. **Bubbles** for the sentences; **Portrait** left or right. For a real conversation with choices, use the [dialogue](/en/docs/dialogues/).

**Dialogue**, **Decision** and **Button** (**Primary** or **Secondary**) have not changed: see [events](/en/docs/interactions/) and [dialogues](/en/docs/dialogues/).

## Questions

The family holds **Question**, **Picture question…**, **Question on a picture…**, **Numeric question**, **Fill in the blanks**, **Match**, **Categorize**, **Put in order**, **Reflection**, **Scale** and **Results**. The player saves the answers with progress and announces them to screen readers. Whether a question is scored, and its feedback, are set in **Properties**, as for any question.

### Question

**Question** is placed as **Single answer** or **Multiple answers**. **Picture question…** (the answers are images) and **Question on a picture…** (the answers are zones on an image) ask for your images first. They are three entries of one question: in the inspector, **Presentation** offers **Text**, **Pictures** and **On a picture**.

On a picture, click to add a **Zone**, drag it to place it. Screen readers hear “Zone 1”, “Zone 2”, until the answer is given, so the picture does not give it away.

### Fill in the blanks

**Fill in the blanks** (*fill in the blank* in Rise) has learners recall words in their sentence: **Typed**, **Menus** or **Word bank**.

- A blank is written between double brackets: `[[answer]]`, with other accepted answers after a bar: `[[DNS|DNS server]]`. Select words and click **Make a blank**: they become one.
- **Extra words** adds distractors to the menus or the word bank. **Accept without accents** makes typing tolerant.
- **Word bank**: learners choose a word, then its blank. Dragging works too, as an extra, with a mouse.
- The score is the share of blanks right. Write feedback for a wrong answer: a check reminds you.

### Match, Categorize, Put in order

These three entries are one block, **Sort** in the inspector (*matching* and *sorting* in Rise; putting in order does not exist there). Its **Presentation** has three choices: **Pairs** (each item has **Its match**), **Categories** (each item has its **Category**) and **Order** (**Items, in the right order**: learners see them shuffled).

- An item, like a target, has a text and may have an image.
- Learners choose an item, then where it goes. Dragging is an extra for those with a mouse; in **Order**, buttons move an item up and down.
- Each item placed right counts: the score is the share of items right, and it is passed to the LMS. Why tap first: [drag and drop: tap first](/en/docs/learning-science/#drag-and-drop-tap-first).
- A check flags items and targets that do not fit together.

### Reflection and Scale

**Reflection** asks a question learners answer in their own words. **Scale** has them place their view or confidence on a scale. In the inspector it is the same block, **Reflection**, as a **Free answer** or a **Scale**.

- **Longest answer** and **Field height** set the field. **An expert’s answer** shows so that learners can compare. **Offer “My answers”** lets them download what they wrote.
- **Scale**: **From** and **To**, with **Lowest means** and **Highest means** (“Not at all”, “Completely”).
- A reflection is never scored. It counts as done once saved, and its answer stays with progress.

## Opening

**Opening** is a screen layout, not a block: the screen opens on its title, a lead paragraph and the reading time. The simplest way is the **Lesson introduction** entry of **Text**, offered on a screen without a layout. Otherwise, choose it in the screen’s **Properties**, on the **Layout** row (**Opening**): a section of the same name sets its presentations and texts.

- **Presentation**: **Title** (the title alone), **Band** (the title over an image, edge to edge), **Side by side** (the title and lead beside the image) or **Cover** (the image fills the first view, the title over it).
- **Eyebrow** stays empty so that the player writes the section and “Lesson 2 of 6”. **Lead paragraph**: a sentence or two on what the lesson brings. **Reading time** shows or hides the page’s duration (“6 min”), computed from its words, media and narration.
- An opening also has room for a question asked before the lesson, a question or a reflection, unscored: it primes the lesson. See [think first](/en/docs/learning-science/#think-first).
- One opening per lesson page, on its first screen.

## Recipes

Recipes are Rise’s ready-made designs: blocks already filled in, where all that is left is to write. Here they are entries like any other, with no indentation and no mark: you find them next to what they are made of.

- **Do / Don’t**, after **Columns**: two columns, what to do and what to avoid.
- **Myth or fact?**, after **Flip cards**: cards whose front asks and whose back answers.
- **Lesson introduction**, after **Title**: the screen opens with its title, a lead and the reading time.
- **Persona**, last in **Text**: a picture, who they are and what they say.

They are not new blocks: once inserted, a recipe is retouched like any other blocks. Your AI can add recipes of your course’s own.

## Transform into…

A block’s **…** menu, and right-click, offer **Transform into…** when the block can become something else: another presentation, or another block that keeps the same items. The menu lists the targets by the block list’s names (**Match**, **Flip cards · One at a time**…), each with a sentence saying what is kept, what is lost and what starts empty. You see it before choosing. A target the block cannot take is greyed out, and hovering it says why. The block keeps its identifier, and one ⌘Z undoes the transformation.

The entry is absent when the block has no target.

## After exploring and Optional

A screen with steps can wait until learners have explored what is above. In the screen’s **Properties**, the **Steps** section says how each step **Opens**: besides **With “Continue”**, **After activities** and **By an event**, there is **After exploring**. **Continue** then waits until the blocks above are explored (every panel opened, every card turned, every step seen) and their activities done. Until then the button is there but locked, and says what is left (“2 panels left to open.”). On a slide, **Next** waits the same way.

**Optional: does not hold back what follows**, in a block’s **Properties**, takes the block out of that wait: a transcript, an extra detail. The setting only shows on a screen that waits for exploration.

Opening is not understanding: put a question right after (see [revealing is not practice](/en/docs/learning-science/#revealing-is-not-practice)).

## What to ask the agent

Your AI knows these blocks and their presentations. It can add them, change the presentation, and turn content into an exercise without rewriting a word:

> On screen 3, turn these panels into flip cards, then add a Sort exercise with the same pairs.

> Give the introduction of lesson 2 an Opening layout, with a band image and a question before the lesson.

> Make “Continue” wait until the four cards of screen 5 have been turned, and make the transcript optional.

The technical details of each block are in [Blocks](/en/docs/developers/blocks/) and [Components](/en/docs/developers/components/), the decision in [D63](/en/docs/developers/decisions/D63/).
